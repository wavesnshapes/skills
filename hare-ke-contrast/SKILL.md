# The golden rule of growth.box: contrast (hare and ke)

In Japanese thought, **ke** is the everyday and **hare** is the rare, festive moment. Hare only means something because ke is steady. growth.box layouts work the same way: a calm, consistent base, broken on purpose by rare, radical moments that carry the design and the story.

## The rule: 80:20
- About 80% of a page is the base mode. About 20% *may* be the contrasting mode. That 20% is an allowance, never a quota. Zero is fine.
- A break has to earn its place: it marks a turn in the story (a reveal, a showcase, a change of pace), never decoration.
- Breaks are radical, not halfway. A contrast that's only a bit different reads as an inconsistency.

## The two axes of contrast
1. **Motion mode.** Switching bento cards (one section per view, switched by scroll or swipe) is a *mode*, not the whole site. A page can mix switching-card stretches with regular scrollable sections in the same layout. If switching is the base, scroll sections are the rare moment, and the other way round.
2. **Width.** Content stays inside the contained content width by default. A full-bleed layout that uses the whole visible width is the rare moment. Typical use: a swiping photo or project gallery with several frames or cards in view escapes the container to fill the screen, while the text around it stays contained.

## Supporting ideas
- **Ma (間):** the space and pause around a moment is part of it. Give a break room before and after it, and don't stack breaks next to each other.
- **Jo-ha-kyū (序破急):** pacing is a slow start, then a break, then a quick close. Put the rare moment where the story turns, not at random.
- **Kire (切れ):** the cut. The switch between modes should be clean and decisive, with no muddy blend.

## How to apply it
1. Decide the page's base mode (switching or scroll) and keep it contained.
2. List the story beats. Choose at most about one in five to be a hare moment.
3. For each hare moment, pick one axis to flip (mode or width), or both for the single strongest beat.
4. Check: is every break justified by the story? Is there ma around it? Does the base still feel like roughly 80%?
5. Build components so mode (switch or scroll) and width (contained or full-bleed) are per-section settings, not hard-coded per page.

## Anti-patterns
- Every section full-bleed, or alternating modes every section, so nothing is special.
- Full width used for text blocks.
- A break with no narrative reason.
- Treating 20% as a target to fill.
